## 02 · How we got here  (2:03)

**[2:03] chapter**

To understand why this launch feels so big, look at the road that led here. This game has been in development for more than a decade.

**[2:11] timeline**  _Source: GameSpot · Variety · GTA Vi Spot (fan site)_

Rockstar revealed the first trailer in December 2023, aiming for 2025. In May 2025, the date moved to May 26, 2026. Then on November 6, 2025, Take-Two announced another delay, this time to November 19, 2026. On May 21, 2026, Take-Two's CEO confirmed that date again on an earnings call. And on October 15, 2026, the launch trailer arrived.

**[2:40] bignum**  _Source: GameSpot · Variety_

The second delay pushed the game back by roughly six months. Rockstar said the extra time would let the team finish the game to its usual standard, and it apologized for the wait. At the time, Take-Two's shares fell by nearly 30 dollars in after-hours trading, then rebounded.

**[2:56] cards**  _General industry context · not specific to GTA 6_

Why do huge games slip? Rockstar has only said it needs more time to polish, but we can look at why this happens in general. Open-world games have enormous amounts of content that must all work together, so late-stage bug fixing can take months. Performance tuning is another big piece, especially making sure the game runs smoothly on base consoles. And console makers require final testing and certification before any game can ship. None of this is a claim about the specifics of GTA 6. It is general industry context.

**[3:28] cards**  _Source: Kotaku · Variety_

Since then, the messaging has been consistent. In May, Take-Two's CEO, Strauss Zelnick, said the company feels really good about November 19. He also said that Take-Two will still market the game heavily, though with less reliance on network television ads than in the past. And as of May, the price and a third trailer had not been revealed. The price has since appeared in pre-order listings.

## 06 · Fact vs rumor  (7:07)

**[7:07] chapter**

Rumors spread fast around a launch this big.

**[7:11] verify**  _Source: Kotaku_

Here is a real example from this year. In the week before the May earnings call, a retailer page appeared to leak a pre-order date, but that turned out to be a mistake. An anonymous forum post then claimed a delay to February 2027, which had no credibility. Then the CEO confirmed November 19 on the call.

**[7:30] checklist**

Here is a simple three-step filter for any GTA 6 headline. One: does it come from Rockstar, Take-Two, or an official store page? Two: does the claim name a source, a date, and a number? Three: does the outlet say confirmed, or does it say reportedly? If a claim fails these tests, treat it as a rumor and wait.
